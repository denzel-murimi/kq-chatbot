# Medical Clearance and Travelling with Medical Needs

## When medical clearance is required
Kenya Airways requires medical clearance before travel if you have had a recent major medical incident, have had surgery in the previous two weeks, have a pre-existing heart or respiratory condition, have thrombophlebitis or a history of pulmonary embolism, or have an unstable mental illness. Clearance is also required if you cannot care for yourself, cannot sit in a normal upright seat, or need in-flight oxygen, a portable oxygen concentrator, a ventilator, battery-powered medical equipment other than a CPAP machine, or a medical procedure during the flight.

## The MEDIF form
Medical clearance is arranged by completing the MEDIF (Medical Information Form for fitness to fly) at least 24 hours before travel. The form must be completed in English, signed, stamped and dated, and it is valid for 7 days from the date of issue. Once cleared, you receive a Passenger Medical Clearance form, which you must carry throughout your journey. A separate MEDIF is needed for the return trip, and you should tell the medical team of any change in your condition before you fly. A fast-track assessment is available within one working day at the JKIA clinic in Nairobi.

## Carrying medication
Carry your medication in your cabin baggage, together with your prescriptions and a doctor's letter, and check the regulations of your destination country beforehand. Medication cannot be refrigerated on board. If you need to inject medication during the flight, tell a cabin crew member.

## Medical equipment and batteries
Medical devices must be IATA-approved and dry-cell battery operated, with enough battery supply for 1.5 times the flight duration. Equipment needed during the flight must be approved by the engineering department at least 24 hours before departure. A portable oxygen concentrator that is carried but not used must have its battery removed and packed separately, unless it has at least two features that prevent accidental operation.

## Oxygen, stretchers and escorts
Medical oxygen is supplied as prescribed and subject to clearance, and a passenger needing oxygen requires an appropriate medical escort. A stretcher case requires at least 48 hours' notice, and a medical escort for a stretcher or oxygen must be notified to the medical team at least 48 hours before the flight. Ground support such as an ambulance or ambulift must be arranged in advance for every part of the journey, including connecting and onward flights.

## Medical service fees
Medical service fees are charged one way. The airport medical review and the passenger medical clearance each cost USD 10 locally or USD 20 internationally. An airside ambulance costs USD 50 and a landside ambulance USD 125. Medical oxygen costs USD 250 per bottle.
